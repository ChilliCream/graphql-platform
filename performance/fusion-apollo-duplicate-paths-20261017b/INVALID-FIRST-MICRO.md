# First joint campaign: invalid arena lifecycle

The first joint campaign completed ten timing launches and stopped on the first allocation launch after its no-GC check failed. It is not a valid normal-lifecycle micro gate. No C3 performance conclusion or HTTP promotion follows from it.

The fixture called `MemoryArena.Dispose()` without `Seal()` for both freshly created arenas on every prepared request. The pinned production source explicitly abandons unsealed pages and tables instead of returning them to their pools (`MemoryArena.cs` lines 15–20, 329–395). Production completion seals arenas before their response owners dispose them (`OperationPlanContext.Complete` and request executor completion). This is a concrete fixture defect, independently confirmed by C1 and C3 reviewers.

All timings, including adverse C3 observations, remain retained. C3 TestQuery-sequence means were 273.528, 270.092, and 270.844 microseconds/request; the four interleaved controls were 170.301, 164.672, 282.616, and 163.198. C3 relative to both adjacent controls was respectively +60.61%/+66.10%, +64.02%/−4.43%, and −4.17%/+65.96%. These are the observed invalid-fixture results, not estimated production deltas. No trimming, tail exclusion, or favorable-control selection was applied.

Within-launch observations frequently contained single 128-request blocks averaging 2–2.8 milliseconds/request among blocks averaging single or tens of microseconds. Whole-case GC counts commonly recorded two to four gen2 collections. Some very large pauses fell inside the measured Execute/Clean scope and others during the excluded Prepare/ReleaseArenas scope. The data cannot establish that every pause was a GC pause, nor isolate an allocation-induced C3 effect from this lifecycle defect. First-five and last-five block means, full control envelopes, adjacent ratios, GC deltas, and input hashes are retained in `results/invalid-unsealed-arena-c3-review.json`.

The allocation attempt used server GC, .NET 10.0.12, CPU affinity 2,3,10,11, 64 warmup requests per case, four observations of 16 requests, and a 512 MiB no-GC region per case. `TryStartNoGCRegion` succeeded, but at least one collection count changed before validation completed. No per-case progress was emitted, so the failing case and the exact cause remain unknown. An unsealed-arena correction is necessary; that source finding alone does not prove the allocation failure's cause. The failed counters do not establish a byte saving.

The corrected fixture must seal arenas, preserve scoped Execute/Clean measurement and the frozen candidate DLLs, record allocation diagnostics per case, and obtain a new exclusive measurement grant. The first campaign remains published diagnostic evidence. Corrected measurements are justified by the demonstrated lifecycle defect rather than an attempt to improve unfavorable results.
